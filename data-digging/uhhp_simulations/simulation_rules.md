A year in our pool

Pre-Auction Draft - look at the prior years roster, subtract all contracts by 1 year. Look up the ages for each player. If they are 26 as of July 1st of the draft year, they are an RFA and the GM retains matching rights. If they are 27 as of that day, they are a free agent.

Pre-Draft Buyout - GMs are allowed to buyout any number of contracts prior to the start of the draft. If there is 1 year remaining in the contract there is no cap hit. If there are 2 years remaining, the is a cap hit created formatted at z-(player name) with a salary of half the salary rounded up to the nearest whole. And if a 3 year is bought out the hit is 2 years with the same salary formula.

Rookie Draft - Based on the draft order which is the reverse standings at the end of the regular season (week 22), if a GM has a draft pick for that year they draft a player for the NHL Draft that occured that summer. Each team is created 3 years of draft picks and those picks can and are often traded. Newly draft players come at no cap hit and can remain on a roster at a $0 contract for up to 3 years. GMs do not active players at the day of the draft ever.

Auction Draft - Players are auctioned based on the same order as the rookie draft. You understand these rules. GMs will apply different strategies. Many will nominate a high value RFA early to force bidget constraints on the owning GM with the hopes of driving down the cost of a player they really want. Each year once enough budget has been spent in the first round the price drops suddenly on players and someone overbids. Then a new price pattern is established. This drop needs to be modeled in. GMs have bid strategies and historical patterns and biases. These should be documented and factored in. Some GMs will make sure they have up to $8 of cap space available after the Auction draft hoping to pluck great players off the waiver wire or accept great players in trade from GMs that made mistakes during the auction. Some GMs spend to the limit of $100. And some accidently go over the budget and have to make difficult choices (this isnt the norm. NoN made that mistake last year).

Waiver weeek - Once the draft is complete the Admins take a few days to process all the transactions. Once that is done daily waiver bidding occurs until the start of the season. GMs must be roster compliant by the first game of the season. Assume all act rationally and do so.

Start of season - Once the season starts there is no more cap constraints and the luxury tax starts. GMs then monitor the weekly performance of players on the waiver and then begin to sign the free agents that signal good performing seasons, as well as youthful players that look to have promising seasons / career starts.

Buyers / Seller - at some point in the season GMs decide if they are going to have a chance at competing and are going to be buyers or if they should be selling for next year. Once that happens they beging to start conversations for trades. Most trades are ones where selling teams can move UFAs with 1 or 2 years remaining in exchange for RFA with upside and control. Draft picks are generally traded as part of deals, never just a draft pick for a draft pick.

Trade Deadline - lately trade flurishes occur before the trade deadline because GMs understand their teams fate way before that. You'll need to predict when taht occurs based on trends in the transactions.

After the deadline the season continues out until the end of the regular season, followed by the elimination playoff round for the Stanely Cup (top 6) and side playoffs for the Coke Cup (bottom 6)
